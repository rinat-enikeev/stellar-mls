Subject: Pre-submission inquiry -- feature: metadata-hiding group registries (case study)

Dear Dr. Voas,

I am Rinat Enikeev, an independent researcher (Tallinn, Estonia); this is a pre-submission inquiry per the CFP guidance. I propose a feature: a postmortem-driven case study of building a metadata-hiding group-membership registry on a public ledger.

Content encryption is a commodity, but group-membership metadata still leaks to at least one operator in most deployed messaging systems. The EncroChat (~60,000 users, 2020) and Sky ECC (~170,000 users, 2021) takedowns were metadata-targeted. Signal's Private Group System is the deployed outlier, but its guarantee requires trusting the running server binary.

The article is a postmortem-driven case study, not a protocol-proposal paper. The system, Onym, anchors group state as a 32-byte commitment on Stellar and gates changes on Groth16 proofs over BLS12-381. It scopes to one of four types -- Anarchy, where any current member can authorize any next state alone -- and frames the contribution as four engineering lessons that generalize. The other three (1v1, Democracy, Oligarchy) are out of scope; Anarchy is the lightest type.

Outline:

- The metadata problem and why existing architectures leave at least one operator positioned to observe
- Onym Anarchy's construction, with formal material as engineering evidence, not as the contribution
- Four postmortems: (a) cost-counting at primitive boundaries; (b) what a soundness theorem does and does not bind; (c) when a Security Considerations bullet is fiction; (d) trusted-setup prerequisites that documentation cannot discharge
- Limitations and open problems

The article intersects four CFP topics: Privacy-Enhancing Technologies, Real-World Cryptography, Sociotechnical Security and Privacy, and Surveillance. The narrative-and-takeaway style fits a postmortem better than a transactions venue.

Testnet only, no production customer, Phase-2 trusted-setup MPC not yet completed. Formal reductions to follow on IACR ePrint before camera-ready. Generative AI was used in drafting; per IEEE policy, that use will be disclosed in the manuscript's acknowledgments section with the required detail.

The draft is ~5,000 words with 15 references -- would this fit the feature track, a department piece, or out of scope?
