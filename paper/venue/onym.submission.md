## 3. Figure count cap and DPI

**Verified** from [IEEE Author Center — magazine submission requirements](https://magazines.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-magazine-article/article-submission-requirements/):

- **Formats.** PS, EPS, PDF, PNG, or TIFF for graphics. Vector preferred (editable original required for line art; IEEE may redraw line art to match magazine standards).
- **Minimum pixel widths.** Column-wide: 1,050 px. Page-wide: 2,150 px.

**Figure count cap: unverified for S&P specifically**. IEEE Network's cap is six figures/tables combined. Current `paper/venue/onym.md` has zero figures; a pre-submission expansion should add at most one or two (a commitment-construction diagram showing the nested Poseidon structure from `§2`, and/or a before/after diagram for the P1 fix in `§4.1`). ## 4. AI-use disclosure form wording

**Verified** from [IEEE Using AI-Generated Content policy](https://ieee-cas.org/publications/using-ai-generated-content-ieee-article) and [IEEE RAS generative-AI guidelines](https://www.ieee-ras.org/publications/guidelines-for-generative-ai-usage/):

> "The use of content generated by artificial intelligence (AI) in an article (including but not limited to text, figures, images, and code) shall be disclosed in the acknowledgments section of any article submitted to an IEEE publication. The AI system used shall be identified, and specific sections of the article that use AI-generated content shall be identified and accompanied by a brief explanation regarding the level at which the AI system was used to generate the content."

**Three additional rules, verified:**

1. AI **cannot** be a co-author. An author must take responsibility for all AI-generated content.
2. AI use **for editing and grammar enhancement only** is outside the policy's intent; disclosure is "recommended" rather than required.
3. Manuscript text under review **must not** be submitted to public AI platforms for review generation; this is a confidentiality breach under IEEE policy.

**Current state of the paper.** The `Use of generative AI` paragraph in `§Acknowledgments` of `paper/venue/onym.md` identifies the AI system ("Claude (Anthropic, 2026)"), names the roles (literature tutor; interlocutor for `§2` and `§4` framing choices), and retains author accountability. This is compatible with the policy.

**One remaining gap** (already open per `paper/onym.v4.review.md` gap 1): the preceding sentence in `§Acknowledgments` thanks "an external code reviewer" — that one is about a human who caught P1 and is fine — but earlier drafts also thanked "the anonymous reviewer whose hard critique shaped the paper's framing," which is Claude in disguise. Verify `paper/venue/onym.md` no longer carries that line (the current file's acknowledgments paragraph reads cleaner than v4.md's, but recheck before submit).

**What to enter in ScholarOne's AI-disclosure field.** Paste the `Use of generative AI` paragraph verbatim, plus one line: "No text in this article was taken verbatim from AI output; the author is accountable for every technical statement."

## 6. Graphical abstract / video abstract requirement

**Graphical abstract: optional, not required** for IEEE S&P under current IEEE Computer Society magazine policy. Specs, verified ([graphical abstract spec](https://eds.ieee.org/images/files/Publications/graphical_abstract.pdf)):

- Size: 660 × 295 px (landscape).
- Format: JPG, < 45 KB.
- Must be derived from a figure in the accepted article, not a new creation.
- Peer-reviewed along with the article if submitted.

**Video abstract: optional**. Specs: ≤ 100 MB, synchronized captions required if the video has audio ([IEEE Access video integration](https://ieeeaccess.ieee.org/authors/video-integration/)).

**Recommendation for this paper.** Skip both for the initial submission. If the paper is accepted and a figure has been added to `§2` or `§4.1` (see item 3), derive a graphical abstract from that figure for camera-ready. A video abstract is not a fit for an experience report of this length.

## 7. Preprint policy — IACR ePrint cross-post

**Verified** from [IEEE Post-Publication Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/post-publication-policies/):

**Pre-submission posting is permitted.** Authors may post preprints on "arXiv.org, TechRxiv.org, or any not-for-profit preprint server approved by the Publication Services and Products Board." IACR ePrint qualifies as a not-for-profit preprint server; posting before submission is standard practice in the crypto community and does not constitute prior publication.

**Post-submission requirement.** The preprint posted pre-submission must be updated to carry the following notice on the initial screen:

> "This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication. Copyright may be transferred without notice, after which this version may no longer be accessible."

**Post-acceptance requirement.** The posted preprint must be replaced with either (a) the full citation to the IEEE-published work with DOI, or (b) the author-accepted version with DOI and the IEEE copyright notice:

> "© 20XX IEEE. Personal use of this material is permitted. Permission from IEEE must be obtained for all other uses, in any current or future media, including reprinting/republishing this material for advertising or promotional purposes, creating new collective works, for resale or redistribution to servers or lists, or reuse of any copyrighted component of this work in other works."

**Recommendation.** Post to IACR ePrint **after** the `§5` artifact-identifier fix lands and **before** ScholarOne submission. Include the "submitted to IEEE for possible publication" notice on the landing page from day one (cheaper than updating later). Keep the ePrint ID for the ScholarOne cover letter.
